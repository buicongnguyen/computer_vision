# Networking and Referrals

Networking works best as technical relationship-building, not a request to skip
evaluation. Start with people whose work genuinely connects to yours.

## High-signal places

- Authors/maintainers of papers, datasets, libraries, benchmarks or talks you
  have actually used and can discuss specifically.
- Former colleagues/classmates and second-degree contacts who know your work.
- CV/robotics/GPU meetups, conferences, reading groups and open-source reviews.
- Recruiters for a clearly matched live role.

## Short outreach structure

```text
Why this person/team: one specific, truthful connection
Your relevance: one sentence with evidence, not a life story
Small request: a focused question or 15-minute perspective
Easy context: portfolio/case-study and exact role link
Respectful exit: acknowledge their time; no pressure
```

Example:

> I used your team’s `[public artifact]` while building `[specific system]` and
> found `[technical observation]`. I’m considering `[exact role]`; my background
> is `[one relevant proof]`. If you have time, I’d value your view on whether the
> team prioritizes `[A]` or `[B]` for this role. Here is a concise case study.

Personalize every factual field. Do not mass-send this template.

## Referral request

Ask only after the person has enough evidence to make an honest judgment. Send
the job link, targeted resume, two-sentence fit summary, location/work status if
relevant, and a graceful option to decline. A referral is their reputation.

## Follow-up

Follow up once after roughly a week unless a date was agreed. Add value when
possible: a result, corrected documentation, thoughtful question, or relevant
public resource. Avoid repeated nudges.

## Long-term proof

Small useful contributions compound: reproduce a bug, improve a dataset check,
write a clear benchmark, answer a technical question, review code, or present a
failure analysis. Never share confidential interview questions or employer work.
