# Senior Leadership and Behavioral Preparation

Behavioral preparation begins with evidence recovery, not polished adjectives.
Use employment records, design docs, incident reports, performance reviews, and
trusted colleagues to restore exact scope and outcomes without exposing
confidential information.

## Build a 12-story bank

Prepare distinct examples for:

1. Ambiguous problem turned into direction.
2. Architecture or technical strategy decision.
3. High-stakes trade-off with incomplete evidence.
4. Disagreement with a peer or leader.
5. Influencing a team you did not manage.
6. Mentoring or raising the engineering bar.
7. Production incident and prevention.
8. Project that failed or missed its goal.
9. Difficult prioritization or saying no.
10. Cross-functional delivery across research/platform/product/hardware.
11. Quality, privacy, safety, or ethics concern.
12. Major measurable launch or cost/performance improvement.

One story can support more than one theme, but a full loop needs enough variety
to avoid forcing the same success into every answer.

## Story worksheet

```text
Headline (one sentence):
Situation: scale, constraints, why it mattered
Task: outcome you personally owned
Actions: 3-5 decisions/actions, ordered; alternatives and collaborators
Result: baseline -> outcome, measurement method, downstream effect
Difficulty: uncertainty, conflict, failure or constraint
Learning: what changed in your later behavior/system
Credit: specific contributions of others
Follow-ups: hardest technical detail; dissent; what you would do differently
Confidentiality-safe version:
```

Target a 90-second first answer, then retain enough technical depth for ten
minutes of follow-up.

## Weak-to-strong transformations

- “I improved accuracy” -> baseline, metric/slices, decision, experiment rigor,
  deployment constraints, outcome, and your ownership.
- “I led the team” -> mechanisms: review structure, roadmap, interface, mentoring,
  conflict resolution, risk removal, and outcome through others.
- “A teammate was difficult” -> shared goal, source of disagreement, evidence,
  your listening/change, decision process, and relationship after delivery.
- “The project failed because requirements changed” -> early signals you missed,
  decisions you owned, containment, communication, and systemic correction.

## Common follow-ups

- What exactly did you do rather than the team?
- What alternative did you reject?
- Who disagreed, and what were they right about?
- How did you know the result was caused by your change?
- What broke or nearly broke?
- What would your teammate say you should improve?
- What lasting mechanism existed after you left?
- Why is this senior-level rather than competent execution?

## Red flags to remove

- Blaming people, vague “communication issues,” or claiming all credit.
- Outcomes without baseline or measurement conditions.
- A conflict story in which you never changed your mind or learned.
- Describing activity instead of a decision and its effect.
- Revealing confidential data, model details, customer identities, or source code.
- Inventing scale or impact. Approximate honestly and label uncertainty.

## Practice score (0-4)

Score relevance, structure, personal ownership, technical depth, measured impact,
leadership mechanism, humility/learning, and follow-up resilience. Pass at average
3 with no category under 2.
