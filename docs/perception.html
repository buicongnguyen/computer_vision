Core system · perception

Turn sensor evidence into decisions you can trust

Perception is not a collection of heads attached to a backbone. It is a timed, calibrated, measurable contract between the physical world and a downstream consumer—with uncertainty and degraded behavior included.

Sensor-to-decision pipeline

Every boundary needs an explicit contract

Debug from left to right; validate safety from right to left.

  1. 01

    Sense

    Capture image, range, Doppler, and inertial measurements with exposure and timestamp metadata.

  2. 02

    Calibrate and synchronize

    Correct intrinsics and distortion; align extrinsics, motion, rolling shutter, scan time, and clock domains.

  3. 03

    Encode

    Normalize the signal and extract features while preserving the resolution, geometry, and detail the task needs.

  4. 04

    Infer

    Predict objects, masks, keypoints, depth, flow, lanes, free space, semantics, or occupancy.

  5. 05

    Associate and fuse

    Combine views, modalities, and history using frame alignment, gating, reliability, and missing-input behavior.

  6. 06

    Represent

    Emit boxes, tracks, vector maps, BEV features, occupancy, embeddings, or a scene state with confidence and age.

  7. 07

    Decide

    Search, rank, inspect, alert, plan, or control under a known deadline and cost of false positive versus false negative.

  8. 08

    Monitor

    Measure data health, output quality, uncertainty, latency, drift, incidents, and fallback activation after launch.

Reverse design

Begin with the decision, not the architecture

If a planner needs collision-relevant occupancy at 20 Hz, a high image-level classification score is insufficient. Work backward from spatial extent, update rate, uncertainty, missed-obstacle cost, and fallback behavior to the representation, fusion strategy, task heads, sensor coverage, and data plan.

Task toolbox

Choose outputs for downstream questions

Objects

Detection

Answers what and approximately where? Use 2D or 3D boxes when instances and compact geometry matter.

  • Core choices: anchor-based, anchor-free, set prediction, open vocabulary.
  • Failure modes: small objects, crowding, truncation, class imbalance, score miscalibration.
  • Consumer risk: box overlap does not guarantee collision-relevant geometry.

Pixels and regions

Segmentation

Answers which pixels belong together? Semantic, instance, and panoptic variants trade identity detail against dense coverage.

  • Core choices: decoder resolution, class weighting, mask assignment, boundary loss.
  • Failure modes: thin structures, rare classes, fuzzy labels, boundary erosion.
  • Consumer risk: high global IoU can hide a dangerous rare-class miss.

Geometry

Depth and 3D

Answers how far and in what spatial configuration? Sources include stereo, active sensing, monocular priors, and multi-view reconstruction.

  • Core choices: metric versus relative depth, scale, representation, uncertainty.
  • Failure modes: reflective surfaces, textureless regions, long range, changed intrinsics.
  • Consumer risk: average depth error obscures near-field geometric bias.

Time

Tracking

Answers which observation belongs to the same entity over time? Detection, motion, appearance, and lifecycle policy jointly determine identity.

  • Core choices: state, motion model, gating, assignment, birth and deletion.
  • Failure modes: occlusion, crossing, ego motion, delayed measurements, duplicate tracks.
  • Consumer risk: unstable identity creates unstable velocity and prediction.

Shared state

Fusion

Answers how should complementary evidence become one spatial and temporal belief?

  • Core choices: raw, feature, BEV, object, or track-level fusion.
  • Failure modes: calibration drift, stale inputs, sensor dominance, conflicting evidence.
  • Consumer risk: extra sensors can increase confidence without increasing correctness.

Scene state

BEV and occupancy

Answers what occupies space around the ego system? A shared top-down state supports fusion and planning but inherits lifting and visibility errors.

  • Core choices: grid extent, resolution, height, unknown versus free, temporal memory.
  • Failure modes: hidden depth error, stale evidence, occlusion, compute growth.
  • Consumer risk: never-observed space must not silently become free space.

Evaluation

A metric is a lens, not the product objective

Report quality, calibration, timing, and slice behavior together.

Detection and segmentation

IoU = |P ∩ G| / |P ∪ G|

Use precision-recall and AP across thresholds, plus per-class, range, size, occlusion, and boundary slices. A single IoU threshold creates brittle pass/fail behavior near the boundary.

Depth and geometry

AbsRel = (1/N) Σ |d̂i − di| / di

Pair AbsRel or RMSE with scale-aware plots, distance bins, surface types, point density, and reprojection error. Evaluate the geometry the product actually consumes.

Tracking and time

Use HOTA, IDF1, identity switches, fragmentation, velocity error, age-to-confirmation, and end-to-end latency. Evaluate at sequence level because frame averages erase lifecycle failures.

Calibration and confidence

ECE = Σb (nb/N)|acc(b) − conf(b)|

Reliability diagrams, NLL, Brier score, risk-coverage curves, and selective metrics ask whether confidence supports a useful operating policy.

Uncertainty

Confidence must change behavior

Aleatoric uncertainty

Ambiguity or noise in the observation: blur, occlusion, range sparsity, or indistinguishable classes. More data of the same quality may not remove it.

Epistemic uncertainty

Uncertainty in the learned model or coverage: a rare object, unseen domain, or insufficient training support. Data diversity and model evidence may reduce it.

State uncertainty

Covariance over position, velocity, orientation, depth, or calibration. Propagate it through transforms instead of attaching an unrelated score.

Policy test

If a confidence score cannot change a decision, why compute it?

Define thresholds through explicit costs, selective prediction, fallback, re-observation, human review, speed reduction, or sensor redundancy. Validate calibration on the slices where the policy will use it.

Continuous improvement

Failure → Evidence → Data → Experiment → Gate → Monitor

  1. Detect

    Capture the incident

    Preserve inputs, outputs, versions, timing, calibration, downstream effects, and surrounding context.

  2. Localize

    Find the broken contract

    Separate sensor, calibration, data, model, threshold, association, fusion, runtime, and consumer failures.

  3. Reproduce

    Create a durable slice

    Turn one anecdote into a versioned scenario definition with positives, counterexamples, and label review.

  4. Change

    Test the smallest hypothesis

    Compare data, loss, architecture, calibration, post-processing, and systems changes independently where possible.

  5. Gate

    Protect global and critical behavior

    Require improvement on the target slice without unacceptable regression, latency, memory, or calibration cost.

  6. Learn

    Deploy gradually and monitor

    Shadow, canary, observe risk and drift, preserve rollback, and feed new failures back into the evaluation set.

System review

Defend the system, not just the network

Scenario A: global AP rises, near-field recall falls

  1. Establish whether sampling or metric weighting hid the regression.
  2. Quantify product cost by distance, class, motion, and confidence.
  3. Trace data, augmentation, resolution, assignment, and threshold changes.
  4. Define a launch-blocking slice and recovery experiment.

Scenario B: fusion improves clean data but fails after drift

  1. Inject extrinsic and timestamp offsets to build a response curve.
  2. Measure whether confidence recognizes the inconsistency.
  3. Compare feature fusion with an independent late-fusion fallback.
  4. Specify online health checks and degraded behavior.

Review rubric

  • Consumer and operating domain are explicit.
  • Frames, timing, units, and semantics are testable.
  • Metrics connect to costs and critical slices.
  • Uncertainty changes behavior.
  • Missing, stale, and contradictory inputs are covered.
  • Deployment, monitoring, and rollback are credible.
