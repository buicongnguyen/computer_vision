How the references are used

One coherent theory-to-systems path

01 · First principles

Images and geometry

Image formation, signal processing, local features, transformations, camera models, calibration, stereo, reconstruction, flow, and tracking.

02 · Learned perception

Recognition to foundation models

Classical recognition leads into optimization, CNNs, detection, segmentation, video, transformers, self-supervision, and multimodal learning.

03 · 3D and autonomy

Neural scenes and BEV systems

Modern 3D representations and embodied perception lead into an original autonomous-perception unit on inverse perspective mapping and BEV fusion.

Important scope note

BEV is an original extension

The surveyed course descriptions do not present bird's-eye-view conversion as a standalone unit. Stanford-style calibration and multi-view geometry provide the mathematical foundation; separately cited primary research supplies modern lift-to-BEV and temporal-fusion methods. The site does not attribute its BEV module to any university course.

Institution 01

Stanford University

Spring 2026

CS231n: Deep Learning for Computer Vision

Curriculum contribution: the modern deep-learning spine: optimization and backpropagation, CNNs, attention and transformers, detection and segmentation, video, distributed training, self-supervision, generative models, 3D vision, vision-language learning, and robot learning.

Its combination of written reasoning and programming informs the theory-plus-code lesson format. Its proposal, staged milestones, final paper, code, and poster inform the capstone checkpoints.

  • Official Spring 2026 schedule
  • Official assignments overview
  • Official project guide

Access: current lecture videos link to Stanford Canvas. This project links to public slides and notes but does not redistribute them.

Spring 2025

CS231A: Computer Vision, From 3D Perception to 3D Reconstruction and Beyond

Curriculum contribution: camera models and calibration, single-view metrology, epipolar geometry, stereo, structure from motion, depth, feature tracking, optical and scene flow, optimal estimation, neural radiance fields, and Gaussian splatting.

This geometry sequence supplies prerequisites for coordinate frames, inverse perspective mapping, and BEV perception.

  • Official course page
  • Official syllabus
  • Official course notes index
  • Official assignments and collaboration policy

Access: the course policy asks students not to consult online implementations. No problem set, code, answer, slide, or figure is reproduced here.

Institution 02

Carnegie Mellon University

Spring 2026

16-385: Computer Vision

Curriculum contribution: a first-principles laboratory progression through filtering and frequency methods, Hough transforms, features, homographies, camera models, two-view geometry, stereo, recognition, neural networks, optical flow, tracking, radiometry, and computational photography.

  • Official course home
  • Official course information
  • Official assignment index
  • Official notebooks and demos

Access: CMU's policy requires assignment repositories to remain private and prohibits publishing solutions. Every public exercise here is newly authored.

Spring 2026

16-720: Graduate Computer Vision

Curriculum contribution: supplementary graduate depth. Publicly indexed current material verifies an implementation-focused treatment of inverse-compositional Lucas–Kanade tracking and background subtraction.

  • Official course site
  • Official instructor page confirming the offering
  • Official current tracking homework

Verification limit: the main site was not consistently accessible when checked, so this project does not claim a complete Spring 2026 syllabus. The homework is linked only as official evidence; none of its content or assets is copied.

Institution 03

University of Michigan

Winter 2022 archive

EECS 498.008 / 598.008: Deep Learning for Computer Vision

Curriculum contribution: the coding and assessment pattern. The sequence moves from classifiers, optimization, backpropagation, and CNN engineering into efficient architectures, detection, segmentation, recurrent models, transformers, generative models, interpretability, self-supervision, 3D vision, and video.

Its six PyTorch assignments, mixed-format midterm, and end-to-end mini-project motivate original MCQs, coding labs, and pipeline projects.

  • Official course home
  • Official detailed schedule
  • Official syllabus and assessment format
  • Official mini-project guide

Access: Winter 2022 recordings are restricted to Michigan users; the course page separately links public Fall 2019 videos. The collaboration policy prohibits online solutions, and none is reproduced here.

Institution 04

Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Current catalog

6.4300 Introduction to Computer Vision and 6.8300 Advances in Computer Vision

Curriculum contribution: the bridge from early, middle, and high-level vision to learned multi-view geometry, differentiable rendering, neural scene representations, correspondence, flow, point tracking, diffusion, contrastive and masked learning, and vision for embodied agents.

  • Official MIT EECS catalog — Vision section

Access: the catalog provides authoritative current subject descriptions, not a license to republish course assets.

Spring 2022 archive

6.819 / 6.869: Advances in Computer Vision

Curriculum contribution: a project-based precedent spanning early through high-level vision and machine learning. Its balance of problem sets and an open-ended final project informs the practice-to-investigation progression used here.

  • Official MIT CSAIL course archive

Access: this is an archived offering and is labeled as such throughout this site.

Institution 05

University of California, Berkeley

Fall 2026 listing

CS 280A: Introduction to Computer Vision and Computational Photography

Curriculum contribution: a practical, from-scratch path through image formation, spatial and frequency processing, pyramids, homographies and warping, calibration, stereo, multi-view geometry, structure from motion, the plenoptic function, and CNN-based image manipulation.

  • Official CS 280A catalog and Fall 2026 listing
  • Official graduate CS C280 catalog

Access: detailed course archives require CalNet, and the Fall 2026 listing says lecture recordings are unavailable. This project uses only public official descriptions.

Institution 06

Georgia Institute of Technology

Spring 2026

CS 6476: Computer Vision

Curriculum contribution: a theory-to-practice discipline spanning image formation, camera geometry and calibration, feature detection and matching, stereo, motion estimation, tracking, recognition, and scene understanding. Its emphasis on understanding and coding basic methods before relying on high-level libraries informs the site's two-stage labs.

  • Official OMSCS course page
  • Official Spring 2026 syllabus

Access: public lesson content requires an Ed Lessons account. The Spring 2026 syllabus says course work products must not be posted publicly, so only independently created material appears here.

Public repository rules

Access, reuse, and attribution

  • Do not copy university slides, diagrams, assignment text, starter code, datasets, answer keys, or recordings unless an item has an explicit compatible license and that license is preserved.
  • Link to an official page instead of mirroring an asset.
  • Cite a source near a close paraphrase and reserve quotation marks for short, necessary quotations.
  • Preserve the license and attribution of separately reused open-source code or data.
  • Do not imply university authorship, affiliation, approval, or equivalence to university credit.
  • Keep all MCQs, labs, tests, solutions, and project rubrics original so this site cannot function as a solution bank for an active course.
