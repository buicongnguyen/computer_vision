Autonomous-driving systems learning flow

Study autonomous driving as a dependency graph. Computer vision supplies calibrated spatial and temporal evidence; localization, prediction, planning, control, simulation, and safety engineering turn that evidence into a closed-loop system.

Central rule

Every learned block still has a physical contract

Name frames, clocks, units, visibility, uncertainty, data age, output horizon, failure behavior, and the downstream consumer. A more capable model does not repair an undefined transform, stale observation, unobservable calibration update, or unsafe controller interface.

Fast support stream

IMU, wheel odometry, GNSS, vehicle state, and map priors support localization and motion compensation. Their timestamps and reference frames are as important as their values.

Feedback closes the graph

Control changes vehicle pose, which changes the next observation. That feedback creates compounding error, recovery, and interaction effects that logged open-loop metrics cannot reproduce.

Sensor front ends

Turn raw signals into timestamped evidence

Do not begin fusion with anonymous tensors. Preserve sensor physics and metadata until their downstream value has been measured.

LiDAR signal → point-cloud flow

emit → detect return → range/signal/return ID → packet time → calibrated beam angles → XYZ → deskew → filter → reference frame

r = c Δt / 2x = r cos φ cos θ   ·   y = r cos φ sin θ   ·   z = r sin φ

Keep ring/beam ID, return identity, intensity or signal field, validity, and acquisition time. A point without frame, units, and time is incomplete evidence.

Stereo depth and uncertainty

d = uL − uR   ·   Z = fB / dσZ ≈ Z² σd / (fB)

Far objects have small disparity, so nearly constant disparity error produces rapidly increasing metric-depth error. Report depth by range, texture, occlusion, and valid coverage—not one average.

Geometry and alignment

Separate projection, calibration, registration, and motion compensation

All four use transforms, but they estimate or apply different quantities.

Ap = ATB Bp λ [u, v, 1]T = K [R | t] [X, Y, Z, 1]T XC = z K−1 p̃

For camera–LiDAR projection use λp̃ = K · T(camera←LiDAR) · p(LiDAR), then require positive camera depth, correct distortion state, image bounds, aligned acquisition time, and an occlusion-aware depth buffer.

Deskew to a named time

pi@r = Tworld←sensor(tr)−1 Tworld←sensor(ti) pi

Interpolate a pose trajectory and declare transform direction, reference time, and extrapolation policy. Curved walls after deskew can indicate clock offset, pose error, or extrinsic error.

Registration choices

  • Point-to-plane ICP / GICP: good initialization, overlap, normals, and local refinement.
  • NDT: distribution-based local alignment with a smoother objective, but still geometry- and initialization-dependent.
  • Global retrieval/features + local refinement: large initial error or relocalization.
  • Continuous-time registration: motion during a scan cannot be neglected.

RGB pixels are not metric 3D points; ICP needs stereo/RGB-D/learned metric depth or cross-modal features and a defensible objective.

Build the transform foundation in calibration, then compare planar and depth-aware conversion in BEV.

Representation decision

Choose the world state from the downstream query

No representation is universally best. Preserve the geometry, uncertainty, topology, and latency that the consumer needs.

i = floor((x − xmin) / vx)   ·   j = floor((y − ymin) / vy)   ·   k = floor((z − zmin) / vz)

Halving all three voxel dimensions can create eight times as many dense cells. Sparse storage removes empty computation, not coordinate construction, hashing, feature-density, or accelerator costs.

Frame tree

earth → map → odom → ego/base → sensors

odom stays locally continuous but may drift; map is globally meaningful but can jump after correction. A controller should not receive an unexplained global discontinuity.

Loop-closure rule

Appearance retrieval proposes a revisit; it does not prove it. Verify correspondences and pose, check degeneracy/inlier support, attach realistic uncertainty, and use a robust or rejectable graph constraint. A false closure can deform the complete map.

Prediction is multimodal

A pedestrian can wait or cross; a vehicle can yield or merge. Evaluate coverage, probability calibration, miss/collision probability, map compliance, and rare modes—not only minimum displacement.

Trajectory ≠ path

A trajectory adds time, speed and acceleration. Collision checks must interpolate densely enough to prevent tunneling and include footprint, actuator delay, dynamics, comfort and unknown space.

Human imitation ≠ safety

Several actions can be safe. Low distance to one logged human trajectory can still collide, violate a rule, fail to recover, or become unstable after the policy changes future observations.

Modern architecture choices

“End-to-end” and “multimodal” describe boundaries—not proof of capability

Current systems increasingly share representations and training signals, while retaining explicit state, temporal memory, safety constraints, evaluators, or fallback paths.

Three meanings of multimodal

  • Multi-sensor: camera, LiDAR, radar, IMU, GNSS or audio.
  • Foundation multimodal: vision/video, language, route/map, ego state and action.
  • Multimodal futures: several plausible predicted trajectories or scene futures.

State which meaning applies. More modalities help only if time, calibration, availability, causal use, and degraded behavior are tested.

What the recent research direction means

Planning-oriented models such as UniAD and VAD connect spatial scene state to motion. Waymo’s EMMA and S4-Driver explore large multimodal models for reasoning and action. NVIDIA’s Alpamayo family explores reasoning vision-language-action models, while Cosmos world models support data generation and simulation.

The defensible conclusion is a trend toward shared learned world representations, temporal context, language/reasoning interfaces, world models, and closed-loop evaluation—not that one opaque model has made geometry, sensor diversity, safety monitors, or system validation unnecessary.

Interpret claims carefully

Reasoning text and photorealistic video are outputs, not safety evidence

An explanation can be plausible but causally unfaithful; a generated scene can look realistic but violate metric geometry, dynamics, sensor physics, or rare-event frequencies. Validate grounding, counterfactual response, geometric consistency, controllability, coverage, and downstream closed-loop behavior.

Evidence ladder

Evaluate from geometric oracles to faulted closed loop

Advance only when each level’s assumptions and failure outputs are explicit.

Level 1

Unit geometry

Transform round trips, projection/back-projection, stereo oracle, voxel boundaries, deskewed synthetic wall.

Gate: known cases pass with declared tolerance.
Level 2

Components

Perception, tracking, localization and prediction metrics with range, weather, motion, class and uncertainty slices.

Gate: failures are decomposed, not hidden by averages.
Level 3

Open-loop planning

Trajectory error, collision proxy, rule/comfort cost, multimode coverage and calibration on fixed logs.

Gate: limitations are stated; no safety conclusion yet.
Level 4

Replay / counterfactual

Deterministic scenarios, reactive actors where supported, policy changes and incident replay.

Gate: actions influence future state.
Level 5

Closed-loop faults

Sensor loss, clock/extrinsic drift, ego-state error, compute delay, map change and recovery.

Gate: fallback and termination are measurable.
Level 6

Operational evidence

Scenario coverage, shadow or controlled-vehicle testing, monitors, rollout/rollback and ODD safety argument.

Gate: evidence matches the deployment claim.

Recommended order

Build one dependency at a time, then close the loop

Do not start with a giant end-to-end model. First create oracles and interfaces that let later learned systems be tested.

Practice and implementation

Turn every arrow into a testable artifact

Knowledge checkAutonomous-driving MCQsEight scenario questions on IPM, stereo, deskew, calibration, SLAM, representations, multimodal models and closed-loop evaluation. ImplementationSix autonomy coding labsLiDAR points, deskew, point-to-plane ICP, RGB–LiDAR projection, trajectory collision and closed-loop replay. System studySensor-to-trajectory capstoneCompare a modular and learned path under calibration, sensor, localization and compute faults.

Minimum experiment matrix

  • camera-only versus camera + range sensor;
  • correct versus perturbed spatial/time calibration;
  • points/pillars or sparse voxels versus BEV/occupancy;
  • modular planner versus one learned alternative;
  • open-loop score versus replayable closed-loop outcome.

Minimum evidence package

  • frame/time/data contracts and dependency diagram;
  • synthetic geometry oracles and real-data visual checks;
  • scenario-sliced metrics with latency/memory;
  • fault detection, degraded behavior and recovery;
  • one conclusion falsified or revised by the experiments.
